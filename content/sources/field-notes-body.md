A year of daily model use leaves you with two piles: prompts that work, and theories about why. These notes are the first pile — the patterns that repeatedly turned a mediocre output into the right one, ranked by how often they actually fix things in practice.

No prompt mysticism, no "act as a world-class expert" incense-burning. Just the moves that transmit intent more clearly, each with a concrete before/after.

## What's inside

- **The seven patterns that move outputs most** — naming the audience, showing one example instead of ten rules, pre-structuring the output, countable constraints, reasoning-before-answer, positive instructions, and where to put the question in a long prompt
- **A debugging method for prompts** — reproduce small, change one thing, read the output as a diagnosis of what got miscommunicated
- **The anti-patterns** — mega-prompts, politeness padding, conflicting constraints, and why re-rolling is a slot machine while revising is an experiment
- **A six-line field checklist** to run before any prompt that matters

## Who it's for

Anyone who uses AI tools more than a few times a week and suspects they're leaving quality on the table. Nothing here needs an API or code — every pattern works in a chat window.

The download packages these notes as a clean PDF, plus a markdown edition you can keep in your own notes app.
